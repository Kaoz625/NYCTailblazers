The method

Build strength and engine at the same time.

Hybrid training means you get stronger and better-conditioned together, on purpose, instead of picking a lane and giving up the other. Done right, you don’t have to trade your deadlift for your mile or your mile for your deadlift.

The catch is that it has to be managed. Heavy lifting and hard intervals pull on the same recovery. Put them on top of each other and both stall. So we sequence the week so the hard days don’t collide, keep most of the conditioning easy, and add weight to the bar and volume to the engine week over week.

  • 01Recovery first. The plan is only as good as what your body can absorb between sessions.
  • 02Keep the systems separate. Max-effort lifting and hard intervals land on different days so one doesn’t blunt the other.
  • 03Progressive overload on both sides. The lifting climbs and the conditioning climbs, tracked and on paper.
  • 04Most cardio is easy. Roughly 80% of conditioning sits at an easy pace; the hard intervals are rare and specific.

Everything runs in blocks: four-week phases stacked into a twelve-to-sixteen-week cycle that shifts the emphasis over time instead of trying to peak everything at once. Base, then build, then peak, then retest.

Programs

Pick the track that fits where you are.

Every track runs on the same engine: standards-based benchmarks, block periodization, and real accountability. Where you start is up to your training history, not your ego.

01

Foundations

Who it’s for
New to training, coming back after a layoff, or anyone who hasn’t trained steadily in six months or more.
What’s inside
Clean movement patterns first — squat, hinge, push, pull, carry — plus an aerobic base built mostly at easy pace. Bodyweight benchmarks (plank hold, push-up count) used as friendly baselines, not punishment.
Structure
4–5x per week, 8–12 week block. Base-phase programming, no maxing out, just enough conditioning to build a habit and a floor.
02 Signature program

Hybrid Athlete

Who it’s for
You already train and you want both: a bigger squat and deadlift and a real engine at the same time.
What’s inside
Concurrent barbell strength (squat, deadlift, press progressions) and conditioning (intervals, rucks, loaded carries, steady-state) sequenced on non-consecutive days. Benchmark days — loaded carry for time, sprint shuttle, overhead lift for reps — used to test and re-test, not to grind you down.
Structure
5–6x per week split. Base → build → peak periodization across a 12–16 week cycle. 1:1 or small-group.
03

Marine Corps Prep

Who it’s for
Poolees, recruits, and anyone training toward the USMC Physical Fitness Test or Combat Fitness Test.
What’s inside
Direct work against the real PFT and CFT events: pull-ups, push-ups, plank, the three-mile run, the movement-to-contact run, ammo can lift, and the maneuver-under-fire shuttle. We test your baseline, program the gaps, and re-test on a schedule. Coached by someone who lived the standard.
Structure
Timeline built around your ship or test date. Standards-first, with running and calisthenics volume managed so you peak when it counts.
04

Fat-Loss & Recomp

Who it’s for
You want to change your body composition and keep your strength while you do it.
What’s inside
Strength training three to four times a week to hold onto and build lean mass, conditioning dosed for calorie burn without wrecking recovery, and nutrition and accountability check-ins on top. Not cardio-until-you-drop — a lifting split with the conditioning that actually fits it.
Structure
3–4x lifting + 2–3x conditioning weekly. Ongoing monthly cycle with regular check-ins.

A week in the plan

What a hybrid week looks like.

A representative base-phase week. Hard days sit apart, easy conditioning fills the gaps, and one day is fully off. As the cycle moves into build and peak, the reps drop, the rest grows, and the intensity climbs.

MondayStrength — lower / fullSquat, deadlift or bench variant, pull-ups. Moderate reps (around 4x6), roughly 90 seconds rest.
TuesdayConditioning / HIITShort hard intervals — sprints, kettlebell swings, burpees. About 20 minutes, work-to-rest near 30s:30s.
WednesdayActive recoveryEasy mobility, a light swim or yoga, 30–60 minutes at low intensity.
ThursdaySkill / benchmarkSport skill or a CFT-style benchmark: loaded carry, shuttle, boxing rounds.
FridayStrength — upper / fullSecond strength session, upper or full-body emphasis.
SaturdayMixed / enduranceLonger steady-state conditioning or a mixed circuit — carries, jumps, deadlifts, pull-ups, running — for volume.
SundayFull restOff. Recovery is part of the program, not a break from it.
Weeks 1–4

Base

Foundational movement patterns, higher reps (around 6), moderate rest (around 90s), and an emphasis on easy-paced conditioning volume.

Weeks 5–8

Build

Intensity rises, reps drop to four or five, rest extends toward two minutes, and interval conditioning sharpens up.

Weeks 9–12

Peak

Heaviest, lowest-rep compound lifts (around 3x4), conditioning at its most specific, then a taper and a retest week.
